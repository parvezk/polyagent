# Writing rules for every application answer

Parvez, Oct 29: v3/v4 prose read as AI-written and could get the application rejected on sight. Sources he set: tropes.fyi/tropes-md, github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop, github.com/vercel-labs/writing-guidelines, and Bryan Cantrill's "The revolt of the reader" and "Your intellectual fly is open".

Rules for drafts in this folder:
- First person, plain words, short sentences. Say what happened, with names and numbers.
- No em or en dashes. No "not X, it's Y" or "X isn't the hard part, Y is". No "Not X. Not Y. Just Z."
- No punchy fragments, no quotable closing line, no colon reveals ("The insight: ..."), no rhetorical questions.
- No stacked groups of three. No bold-first bullets in answers.
- Banned words: delve, leverage, seamless, robust, streamline, empower, landscape, ecosystem, paradigm, transformative, bottleneck, plus filler (just, really, truly, actually, simply, fundamentally).
- No tech-stack lists. Reviewers read for person, problem, user, progress.
- No invented concept labels, no vague sources, no claims that can't be checked.
- Evidence beats adjectives: his Jules queue stuck at "Needs clarification" says more than any description of a pain.
- Agents edit; Parvez's voice wins. When he rewrites a line, keep his version.

Added Oct 31 (Parvez): plain is not the same as flat. v6 read as mundane and "lacks soul." Answers must sound like a founder who cares about the problem: say what PolyAgent is up front, use the frontier terms reviewers know (multi-agent orchestration, control panel, human-in-the-loop), lead with wins, and put vision and passion in "Anything else." Keep each section on its topic. Commentary goes in an appendix, never above the answers.
